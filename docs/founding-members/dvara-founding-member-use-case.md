# Dvara and MSME Catalyst: a member, not a founding backer

Internal briefing for Dvara’s leadership and for the MSME Catalyst secretariat. It is not a page for msmecatalyst.org. Prepared on 27 November 2026 from the public site, this repository, and the primary Dvara, CARE, FMO, and Northern Arc documents listed in section 10.

The first draft of this note found no Desktop folder. The paths checked are in section 10. The folder that was meant is the separate repository `github.com/priyashmitaguha/msmecatalyst-documents.` (the name includes the trailing period). That repository was inspected after the first draft. It is a funding proposition and a set of advisor agreements. It uses "founding" and "member" as two different words. Nothing in it names Dvara. This note does not rest on a Windows Desktop having been opened.

Every material figure below carries the document it comes from and the date that document gives it. Group-level facts and Dvara KGFS facts are kept apart. Where two Dvara documents disagree, both figures are shown.

## 1. The ask

The request was that Dvara become a "founding member." The documents now in hand use two different words. This note does not collapse them into one product.

**Founding**, in the brochure, means a founding backer. `BROCHURE - INDIVIDUAL.pdf` is a 17-page PowerPoint export dated 5 October 2026. The footer is "MSME Catalyst | Operated by Digital Growth Infrastructure Foundation." The title slide reads "Founding Support Proposition" and "Clusters are growth infrastructure." The closing slide reads "FOUNDING BACKERS BUILD THE SYSTEM" and "BECOME A FOUNDING BACKER." A speaker note on the companion pptx, which is a speaker note and not published copy, says: "Closing slide for individual founding backers. Funding supports programme execution and evidence for replication." and "No financial return is promised." Another speaker note says: "Programme funding is sought upfront. No milestone-based releases or fixed quarterly reporting frequency are proposed." The brochure states no amount. It is written for individuals. It does not name Dvara.

**Member**, in the advisor agreements, means an institution in the MSME lending ecosystem. The same sentence appears in the Ramgopal Subramani agreement, in both blank drafts, and in Bonani Roychoudhury's letter: "Its membership comprises banks, non-banking financial companies, micro-finance institutions, housing finance companies, fintechs, technology firms, professional services firms, and international organisations that support the MSME lending ecosystem." The letter inviting U. K. Sinha describes a consortium "including Technology Provider, Bureau, potentially NBFC, Banks as well." "Potentially NBFC" means an NBFC member is still prospective.

The institution that should join as a member is Dvara Kshetriya Gramin Financial Services Private Limited, in the Lenders category on the public form. It holds the rural and nano/micro book, the branches, and the suitability practice. Holdings is the wrong member: its annual return for the year ended 31 March 2025 does not list KGFS as a subsidiary or an associate, and CARE's note of 30 March 2026 records Holdings at 1.3 percent of KGFS after the May 2025 infusion. There is a further reason in the organisation's own papers. The membership sentence names non-banking financial companies and micro-finance institutions, and the Sinha letter still says "potentially NBFC," so an NBFC member would be early. Northern Arc Capital Limited is not that member. On 31 March 2025 it had no promoter, and Holdings held 5.14 percent as a public shareholder.

KGFS should not be asked to be the founding backer. On 31 December 2025 it had reported a nine-month loss. CARE downgraded it on 30 March 2026. Asset-quality covenants were breached on 72.1 percent of debt facilities outstanding as on that December date. Funding a Section 8 platform is not a use of that balance sheet. If the group wants a founding-backer conversation, the only honest candidates are a mission entity that actually makes grants. These sources do not show one. Dvara Trust is the promoter CARE names. Dvara Research Foundation is a not-for-profit research institution. Neither the brochure nor the advisor agreements shows a grant budget for this programme. The founding-backer door is not recommended for KGFS, and it is not evidenced for Trust or Research. This note does not name a cheque size. It does not say that Holdings should write one.

Dvara Research's useful role is the brochure's second founding-support item and its fourth pillar: a research programme on why MSME and export-led segments succeeded or failed, and the shared evidence system, including the outcome scorecard. That is a working contribution inside a KGFS membership, or a separate research partnership. It is not itself a founding-backer gift unless Research's board decides to fund. These documents do not show that decision, and this note does not assume it.

Dvara Solutions still fits the membership sentence's "fintechs, technology firms" if it ever applied on its own. That is not the ask. One application, by KGFS, remains the clean structure. Solutions stays a documentation path that a lender owns.

The first-year work, if KGFS joins as a member, is still one Tamil Nadu catchment that KGFS nominates, described as a cohort with whatever anchor actually exists there, plus the suitability method, a lender-owned co-lending file, and Research's work on the scorecard. The application should not request a council seat, a fee waiver, or the founding-backer label. Directors of Digital Growth Infrastructure Foundation can receive an approach. The published Partnerships and Membership role is still "Appointment in progress." Both are true. Filing still waits until the KGFS board and Dvara Trust agree that KGFS is the member, KGFS names the catchment, and a neutrality protocol is written.

## 2. MSME Catalyst in its own words

MSME Catalyst is the public name of a platform operated by Digital Growth Infrastructure Foundation, a Section 8 not-for-profit company (CIN U94990MH2026NPL468930), with its registered office at WeWork 247 Park, 13th Floor, Vikhroli Corp, Mumbai 400079. The site describes it as “India’s neutral convergence layer for MSME growth.”

The home page states the problem in one line: “India does not lack MSME support. It lacks convergence.” The same page says the platform “makes existing support work together around real MSME clusters and business journeys” and “brings anchors, MSMEs, lenders, technology providers, capability institutions, legal and dispute-resolution partners and government into one accountable operating model.” The About page states the purpose as making “existing MSME infrastructure usable on the ground — by converging market demand, cash-flow support, capability, capital pathways and intelligence around live clusters.”

The home page names four connected gaps. They are quoted here as published:

| Gap | The site’s heading | The site’s description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Cash-flow gap | Receivables and payment certainty. |
| 2 | Market gap | Demand-side linkage and buyer access. |
| 3 | Capability gap | Technical, managerial, digital and legal handholding. |
| 4 | Growth-capital gap | Preparation, documentation and access pathways. |

The growth-capital gap, in the site’s own words, is preparation, documentation, and access pathways. It is not a pool of money the platform lends.

The home page says support already exists, and that “they often reach the enterprise separately, on different timelines, through different doors.” What the platform says it does is hold a shared cluster operating framework, convene institutions, prepare enterprises and cases, coordinate referrals, track outcomes, and publish aggregated execution insight. The same section says: “MSME Catalyst is the neutral layer that convenes, prepares and tracks. It does not lend, underwrite or adjudicate — independent institutions retain their own eligibility, commercial and legal decisions.”

The hard boundaries, in the site’s language, are these.

- “MSME Catalyst does not own the lender decision, the legal outcome, the customer relationship or the participant’s data.” It “holds the shared operating framework and the aggregated evidence — on behalf of the whole ecosystem, not any one participant.”
- The published footer: “MSME Catalyst does not lend, underwrite, provide legal advice, adjudicate disputes, operate an ODR platform, guarantee recovery, or replace existing schemes, courts, arbitrators, universities or implementation partners.” Lenders, ODR providers, and other institutions “retain their own eligibility, commercial, legal and operational decisions.”
- The Approach page: “MSMEs remain free to choose providers. No participant gets exclusive access or preferred tie-ins. Neutrality is not a slogan here; it is an operating rule.” The home page’s public count is “0 exclusive tie-ins or lock-in.”
- The Cluster Capability Centre “is not a lender, college, ODR platform or legal service.” Its finance function is “Readiness and documentation — not lending decisions, which stay with lenders.” Its legal function is “Case preparation and neutral referral — not legal advice or adjudication.”
- ODR support organises documents and refers to an independent provider. The platform “is not an ODR platform, law firm, mediator, arbitrator or court,” and it “does not provide legal advice, compel participation, collect money or guarantee recovery.” The statutory MSEFC route “remains available and is not replaced.”
- Terms of use: a referral “is neutral. MSMEs retain choice of provider. Providers retain all their own decisions.”
- Donor funding “does not buy influence over individual lending decisions, ODR referrals, policy positions or case outcomes.”

The operating method is published as four steps: start with demand (anchors, buyers, products, supply chains, markets); build a Cluster Capability Centre; use shared scorecards of readiness, payment friction, capability needs, market linkage, and capital pathways; protect neutrality. Four programme pillars are named: Awareness and Readiness (including Credit Ki Kaksha, Rating Ki Kaksha, and Digital Dost); Receivables and ODR Support (including a Cluster Recovery Cell for case preparation); Capital and Market Pathways; and Cluster Intelligence. The Reports, Blogs, and Events pages are still CMS placeholders (“title to be added”). No live pilot cluster is named on the public site.

Membership, in the site’s words, “is a working role inside cluster infrastructure” and “is institutional, invitation-led and outcome-oriented — not generic logo placement or event access.” It is for banks, NBFCs, and regulated financial institutions; fintechs, TSPs, LSPs, and data and infrastructure providers; rating and ecosystem institutions; anchor corporates and market-access organisations; and funders. The application form uses the categories Lenders, Fintechs, Infrastructure, Anchors, Ecosystem Institutions, ODR Providers, and Donors and Funding Partners. Submissions “are reviewed by the Secretariat and managed in the membership CRM.” The public member wall is generated only from records that are active, paid, and covered by logo consent. The CRM schema in this repository has a fee field. The public site does not publish a fee.

The Governing Council on the public About page is still ten seats, and each card still reads “Member to be announced.” The seats include Banking or Financial Institution Member, NBFC / Alternative Lender Member, and Fintech / Digital Infrastructure Member, along with anchor, MSME, capability, legal and receivables, and three independent seats. Secretariat roles, including Partnerships and Membership, are published as “Appointment in progress.” The site has not caught up with the brochure.

The 5 October 2026 brochure lists four people under the heading “Advisory Committee and Governing Council Members”: R. Gandhi, former Deputy Governor, RBI; Sunil Shrivastava, former DMD, SBI; Pramod Rao, former Executive Director, SEBI; and Ramgopal Subramani, former COO and Founding Member, Perfios. That heading does not say which person sits on which body, and this note does not invent a seat for any of them. The letter inviting U. K. Sinha says that Mr R S Gandhi, Mr Sunil Srivastava, and Mr Pramod Rao “have joined our advisory board and the governing council.” The brochure spells the second name Shrivastava; the letter spells it Srivastava. Ramgopal Subramani’s agreement makes him Category A Chair / Co-Chair Advisor from 1 May 2026, for an initial term of six months. U. K. Sinha was invited as an Advisor for strategic counsel. Bonani Roychoudhury has a six-month advisor letter. Ramgopal’s “Founding Member, Perfios” is his former title. It is not a precedent for this membership, and this note does not import Perfios’s business.

The letters are signed for Digital Growth Infrastructure Foundation. Prashant Mahagaonkar, Director, signs them, except Bonani Roychoudhury’s letter, which is signed by Govind Dichwalkar, Director. The CIN on the letterhead is U94990MH2026NPL468930, the same CIN as the site. Directors of the Foundation can receive an approach. The published Partnerships and Membership role is still unfilled. Both statements stand. No document in the documents repository sets a membership fee, a founding-member fee, or a founding-member benefit. Amounts in the advisor agreements are advisor billing. They are not a membership price, and they are not repeated here.

**“Founding” and “member” are not one product.** The public site, this website repository, and `CONTENT-TO-FILL.md` do not define a founding-member product, fee, or benefit list. The brochure and the agreements, which are the templates, use the two words apart. Founding, on the 5 October 2026 proposition, is a founding backer: an individual who funds execution, with no financial return stated and no ticket size stated. Member, in the agreements, is an institution of the kind named in the membership sentence, which expressly includes non-banking financial companies, micro-finance institutions, fintechs, and technology firms. Joining as a member while the public council cards are still blank, and while the operating model is still a proposition, means helping to design the shared cluster framework rather than only consuming a finished one. That timing is not a grant of a council seat, a logo privilege beyond the ordinary consent rules, a fee waiver, or a preferential lending right. It is also not the founding-backer role. None of those extras is published, and this note does not create them.

The brochure’s own build, labelled here as the 5 October 2026 proposition and not as a live operating result, is slide 11, “FOUNDING SUPPORT: FUNDING EXECUTION,” under “What your backing would build”: (1) a small execution team and a dedicated Secretariat; (2) a focused research programme on why MSME and export-led segments succeeded or failed; (3) 4–5 anchor-led Capability Centres, each with a committed anchor, a defined cohort, a clear business problem, and local partners; (4) a shared evidence system of baselines, a capability-centre playbook, and a common outcome scorecard. The model on that proposition is “a defined enterprise cohort, a committed anchor and a measurable business problem.” Enterprise pathways on the same proposition differ: micro (resilience, cash-flow visibility, documentation); small (quality, productivity, buyer relationships, receivables, suitable working capital); ready-to-expand (governance, MIS, market access, debt or equity readiness). The four pillars on the brochure are Awareness and readiness; Faster receivables; Capital and market access; and Reports, research and mapping. They are the proposition’s labels. The live site’s four programme pillars remain the public description.

Other proposition facts, each from that 5 October 2026 text and none of them a reported operating result: Pillar 2 includes cluster-led payment support, an ODR recovery pathway, and “Business EMI Protection,” which the slide itself calls “Early-stage concept, not a product.” Pillar 3 says market access makes a firm more creditworthy, and that lender pathways “route suitable cohorts to the right bank channel.” The same slide says India has 275 active Central and State schemes, 86 carry a credit facility, about ₹24,000 crore went unabsorbed in FY25, and in 20 of 23 credit-linked subsidy schemes support follows a bank sanction. The slide says this data is compiled from public secondary sources and is not independently verified. That caveat stays attached to the figures. The Capability Centre finance function on the proposition includes “support for TReDS onboarding and follow-through.” MSME Catalyst coordinates. It does not adjudicate. The PDF’s first-year shape is a three-year programme: months 1–9 select and establish; months 3–15 launch and test; months 11–30 measure; months 24–36 evaluate. The companion pptx has an overlapping later draft of a five-year plan. The PDF is the circulated text. The pptx timeline is an unreconciled later draft. Neither is an approved board plan. Speaker notes say the gates remain subject to programme approval.

The brochure’s sentence that lender pathways “route suitable cohorts to the right bank channel” is proposition copy. It is not a licence to make one NBFC the channel. The live site still says MSMEs remain free to choose providers and that no participant gets exclusive access or preferred tie-ins. A stricter sentence sits in `CONTENT-TO-FILL.md` as unpublished worksheet text for the footer: the platform “does not … allocate leads, route MSMEs to preferred lenders.” The live footer does not yet say that. This note treats the live site as the public rule, and treats the worksheet line as the safer operating rule for any Dvara participation. Business EMI Protection and TReDS onboarding remain Catalyst concepts. KGFS does not build them.

## 3. Dvara as it is today

Dvara is a group of institutions around one published mission. It is not one lender, and it is not one cap table.

### Mission

Dvara Holdings’ website states: “Our mission is to ensure that every individual and every enterprise has complete access to financial services.” Dvara KGFS’s website uses the same mission for the trust that incorporated it, and then states KGFS’s own mission as maximising “the financial well-being of every individual and every enterprise by providing complete access to financial services in rural India.” Dvara Research’s website states a related and narrower mission: every low-income household and every small enterprise should have “complete access to suitable financial services and social security.”

The word that matters for this note is “suitable.” KGFS’s company-overview page, which is undated and which still carries an older branch count (see below), describes a “Wealth Management” approach in which “a financial well-being report, which serves as a tool to assess the suitability of a customer’s chosen product, is created by gathering information on a household’s assets, liabilities, income, expenses, and financial goals.” The product-philosophy page organises that approach as Plan, Grow, Protect, and Diversify. FMO’s MASSIF note on 2025 capacity-development contracts describes a grant to update that tool: a Business Intelligence platform, a Customer App, and “the updated Financial Wellbeing Report (FWR 2.0),” in support of a shift “from group-loan-dominated lending to a more diverse individual and MSME portfolio.”

That is the group’s own narrative for FY2024-25. It is not a statement of control.

**Northern Arc Capital Limited** is a listed company (NSE: NORTHARC). Its shareholding pattern for the quarter ended 31 March 2025 shows promoter and promoter-group holding at nil. Dvara Holdings appears in that filing as a public “Bodies Corporate” shareholder: 8,299,258 shares, 5.14 percent. The FY2024-25 impact report still writes Northern Arc into Cohort 1 and describes its own outreach, including “formal credit access for over 118 million individuals” and MSME products such as loan against property and supply-chain finance. Those are Northern Arc facts inside a Dvara narrative. They are not assets KGFS or Holdings can commit to MSME Catalyst. This note does not use a later, unofficial shareholding series. The last official pattern opened here is 31 March 2025.

**Dvara KGFS**, legal name Dvara Kshetriya Gramin Financial Services Private Limited, is the rural and nano/micro credit company. CARE’s note of 30 March 2026 describes it as a non-deposit-taking NBFC, incorporated in August 2008, “promoted by Dvara Trust,” with day-to-day management under a board that includes representatives of “the holding trust.” The company’s website says the same incorporation story and prints CIN U65991TN1993PTC024547. FMO’s disclosure of 28 February 2025 says the company “has been operating in India since 2013.” The 1993 year inside the CIN, the 2008 incorporation statement, and the 2013 operating statement are not reconciled in the sources opened for this note. After the May 2025 infusion, CARE (both the 30 June 2025 note and the 30 March 2026 note) records the shareholding as: Dvara Trust 28.3 percent, Accion Africa-Asia Investment Company 22.0 percent, LeapFrog Financial Inclusion India (II) Ltd 20.3 percent, Abler Nordic Fund IV KS 17.0 percent, Stakeboat Capital Fund 5.3 percent, Sparkassen International Development Trust GmbH (SIDT) 5.6 percent, and Dvara Holdings 1.3 percent. Holdings’ 1.3 percent is why Holdings is the wrong legal member for a commitment that depends on KGFS’s book, branches, or credit policy.

**Dvara Research Foundation** (CIN U67190TN2008NPL068733), incorporated 4 August 2008, is described on its website as “an independent, non-partisan, not-for-profit policy research institution.” Its MGT-7 for the year ended 31 March 2025 lists no holding, subsidiary, or associate companies, and records promoter shareholding of 100 percent under the category “Trust,” with two promoter shareholders. It is the institution that does household-finance and customer-protection research. It is not the lender, and it is not a Holdings subsidiary.

**Dvara Solutions** is the technology company. Holdings holds 21.79 percent and classifies it as an associate, not a subsidiary. The FY2024-25 impact report says Solutions “strengthened digital infrastructure for India’s inclusive finance ecosystem” through PERDIX (loan origination and management) and PERDIX LP, described as middleware for co-lending and partnership books, including “compliance with RBI co-lending guidelines without altering existing technology stacks.” In that year, the report says, 28 financial institutions used its platforms, which “facilitated the management of nearly ₹16,000 crore in assets under management” and “supported close to 800,000 customer accounts,” processing “almost one million transactions every month.” The public site lists a “BC & Co-Lending System” among PERDIX modules, and SME / MSME lending among the asset classes. Solutions sells that infrastructure to lenders. It does not, in these sources, own KGFS’s credit decision.

Other named companies — Health Finance, Smartgold, E-Registry, Money, and the Venture Studio — sit in the inclusion story. They are not the institutions this membership would put to work in year one, and this note does not fold them into the ask.

### Who does rural, nano, and micro-enterprise credit

That is KGFS, and only KGFS among the institutions above. CARE’s 30 March 2026 note says the company “is primarily engaged in lending loans through JLG model” and also provides micro-enterprise loans, a consumer-durable loan, jewel loans, a crop loan, and a personal loan. Enterprise loans have a separate credit team. The branch is the origination point, covering a radius of up to 25 km, supported by hubs. Insurance is distributed through third-party tie-ups (IRDAI corporate agency CA0646 is printed on the website). The 30 March 2026 note describes five channels: 2,845 wealth managers and branch managers, a KGFS Direct tele-calling team of 60 or more, the MyKGFS WhatsApp chatbot, 10,300 or more “KGFS Mitra” agents, and 421 branches, with a customer app still being built.

The FY2024-25 impact report, for the year then closed, reports 570,747 active customers, of which 534,132 (93.58 percent) were women and 423,466 lived in rural areas; ₹2,201.9 crore of AUM and FY25 revenue of ₹604 crore; 10,304 digital agents across 25,818 villages and 116 districts; and 427 branches in 11 states, with new operations in Andhra Pradesh and “the groundwork for expansion into Telangana.” It describes enterprise loans as “larger ticket, cash flow-based loans” for nano and micro-enterprises “operating in vibrant ‘High Street’ market clusters,” underwritten with a “KGFS Score” and with account-aggregator cash-flow analysis. It also says growth followed “a dense cluster approach, prioritizing meaningful market penetration before increasing scale.”

The company’s own About page still says “we are now present in 10 Indian states through 395 branches.” That sentence is older than the CARE and BusinessLine figures above. It is not used as the current footprint.

**Credit quality, because it is part of what “today” means.** CARE downgraded KGFS on 30 March 2026 to CARE BBB; Stable on long-term facilities and instruments, and CARE A3+ on short-term instruments, from CARE BBB+; Negative and CARE A2. As on 31 December 2025, GNPA was 7.31 percent and NNPA 3.27 percent (8.53 percent and 3.91 percent on 31 March 2025). Net stressed assets (NNPA + standard restructured assets + security receipts) were 37.42 percent of net worth, against 17.36 percent on 31 March 2025. Reported profit after tax was ₹1.18 crore in FY25 (the narrative rounds this to ₹1 crore) and a loss of ₹16.65 crore in the nine months to 31 December 2025 (the narrative rounds this to ₹16 crore). CAR was 24.91 percent and Tier-1 CAR 17.70 percent; tangible net worth was ₹372 crore; on-book gearing was 4.30 times. Those capital ratios were supported by equity of about ₹28 crore (euro 3 million) from SIDT and tier-II capital of about ₹42.5 crore (USD 5 million, 10-year subordinated debt) from FMO, both in the nine months to 31 December 2025. CARE also records that financial covenants, mainly on asset quality, had been breached on 72.1 percent of debt facilities outstanding as on 31 December 2025, and says it expects continued lender support and does not anticipate recall. RBI-definition microfinance was less than 4 percent of AUM.

This credit picture is a reason for a careful membership. It is also the context in which the shift toward enterprise and livelihood loans is happening. The use case does not ask MSME Catalyst to bless the book, to improve the rating, or to send enterprises to KGFS so that AUM can recover.

### Co-lending, as a KGFS fact and a Solutions fact

## 4. Why the fit is specific

The weak argument is that Dvara lends to small firms. Many NBFCs do. The argument that is specific to these two institutions is that an enterprise in a cluster fails when demand, receivables, capability, and capital arrive separately, and that Dvara’s own model already refuses to treat the loan as the whole of the household’s problem.

KGFS enrols a household, builds a financial well-being picture from assets, liabilities, income, expenses, and goals, and uses that picture to test suitability. The loan that is growing inside the book is a livelihood or enterprise loan, cash-flow based, aimed at nano and micro-enterprises in rural and high-street markets, not only a group loan for consumption smoothing. Around that loan, the group has spent years on the institutional layer: Research on suitable finance and customer protection, Solutions on the plumbing of co-lending and partnership books, and a branch architecture that KGFS itself calls a dense cluster. MSME Catalyst’s published belief is the same shape of problem from the other side. The enterprise should not have to stitch the doors together. The platform will hold the frame and the evidence, and will not own the credit decision.

Mapped onto the four gaps, the assets are these.

**Cash-flow and receivables.** KGFS already looks at household cash flow, including through account aggregators, and it lives with the consequences when that cash flow breaks: the JLG stress, the guardrails, the ARC sale, the rating. What a branch cannot convene is the buyer. Catalyst’s published cash-flow work is payment certainty, a Cluster Recovery Cell that prepares invoices, purchase orders, and delivery proof, and a neutral referral to an independent ODR provider, without replacing MSEFC and without guaranteeing recovery. For a high-street or village enterprise, the missing piece beside a KGFS enterprise loan is often the receivable from a buyer the branch does not sit with. That is the gap Catalyst is built to hold. KGFS would meet it as one lender in a cluster, with its own collections and its own duty on suitability, while the buyer-side friction is prepared for whatever route the enterprise chooses.

**Market.** Catalyst says it starts with demand: anchors, buyers, products, and supply chains. KGFS’s enterprise loans follow enterprises that are already trading — the impact report’s high-street nano and micro-enterprises, and the customer stories of shops, dairy, and village trade in Tamil Nadu, Odisha, Karnataka, and Chhattisgarh. Those stories show the customer type. They do not show a buyer programme. Catalyst’s market gap is the part of the model a rural NBFC does not own: the anchor at the other end of the order. A founding role would put KGFS’s existing density next to that demand work, in a catchment KGFS already serves, rather than asking KGFS to invent a new geography.

**Capability.** Catalyst’s third gap is technical, managerial, digital, and legal handholding, through a centre that coordinates and does not replace a college or a lender. KGFS’s published capability is different and useful: wealth managers who build a household plan, digital agents, a financial well-being report, and, on the research side, a body of work on how low-income households actually manage money and how customer protection fails. Research’s current programme includes household money management, consent in digital lending, and monitoring of customer protection in microfinance. That is a method for enterprise readiness. It is not a substitute for the handholding Catalyst says a cluster still needs, and it is not a licence for Research to underwrite.

**Growth capital.** Catalyst defines this gap as preparation, documentation, and access pathways. KGFS is one such pathway, for a ticket and a geography — rural and high-street nano and micro enterprises, still majority-unsecured, with a secured share of about 2 percent — that a typical commercial-bank MSME desk and a typical digital personal-loan fintech do not both occupy. Solutions’ co-lending middleware, and KGFS’s own move into BC and co-lending with one unnamed NBFC already live as of the 30 March 2026 note, is a way for a second balance sheet to participate without Catalyst choosing it. The credit decision, the price, and the suitability duty stay with the regulated lender that actually books the loan.

**What Catalyst gains that it cannot get from a generic NBFC or a generic fintech.**

A generic NBFC can offer a credit appetite and a logo. A generic fintech can offer a journey and a webhook. Neither, on the public record, brings all of the following at once.

- An operating suitability practice for rural households, already described by KGFS as a financial well-being report and already being rebuilt, on FMO’s account, as FWR 2.0, aimed at individual and MSME lending rather than at a group-loan monoculture.
- Branch density in the places the enterprises actually are. Tamil Nadu alone was about 46 percent of KGFS AUM on 31 December 2025, and the public locator, whatever its gap against the CARE branch count, lists 161 Tamil Nadu centres. The other ten states are real and smaller. A generic lender that wants a metro MSME book does not bring this map.
- A research institution that is legally outside the lender, states its independence as the condition of speaking for the low-income household, and has a published mission of suitable finance. Catalyst’s scorecard — readiness, payment friction, capability, market linkage, capital pathways — needs that kind of criticism while the scorecard is still being designed. A lender marking its own homework does not provide it. Research can, only if its independence is written down and kept.
- Technology that other lenders already use for partnership books. If the cluster needs a co-lending or BC file, Solutions is a vendor with a stated co-lending product, not a reason for Catalyst to build or own one. That keeps the platform out of the lender’s system, which is what the site says it wants.
- A group that has already described the last two years as a test of whether inclusion survives stress, and that has answered by cutting JLG growth and putting more of the book into livelihood and enterprise loans. Catalyst’s thesis is that capital without the other three gaps is how enterprises fail. KGFS is living the credit half of that sentence in public, including the losses and the downgrade. A member that has already had that experience is more useful to the design of the framework than a lender that arrives only to distribute loans into a finished cluster.

**What KGFS cannot convene alone, which is what membership is for.** A wealth manager in a 25-kilometre branch radius can see the household. The wealth manager cannot require a buyer to pay on time, cannot be the neutral party that prepares a receivable for ODR, cannot be the academic or skilling partner, and cannot publish de-identified cluster evidence without being read as marketing the book. Those are the jobs the site assigns to the platform. They are also the jobs that make an enterprise loan safer to originate, which is in KGFS’s interest and is still not a right to the customer.

The fit has a boundary inside it. Catalyst’s model starts with demand and with anchors. KGFS’s model starts with the household and the village branch. The first-year design has to hold both, or the cluster will become either a lending camp or a buyer event with no credit path. Section 5 is written to hold both.

## 5. What Dvara would do in the first year

Five contributions. Each one stays inside Catalyst’s neutrality. None of them is a founding-backer cheque, a right to a council seat, a right to exclusive origination, or a transfer of customer data. The catchment is Tamil Nadu because that is the state the sources support as KGFS’s largest geography. This note does not name a district cluster, a product cluster, or a city. Customer stories on the KGFS website are set in places such as Sivakasi, Villupuram, and Tirunelveli. A story is not a density study. No source opened here supports a Dvara role in a Pune auto-component cluster, and none is proposed.

The 5 October 2026 proposition designs each Capability Centre as “a defined enterprise cohort, a committed anchor and a measurable business problem,” and it says backing would build 4–5 such centres. That number is what a founding backer would fund. It is not a delivery obligation this note puts on KGFS. The member contribution is one catchment, described in that cohort language, inside the PDF’s circulated three-year shape (months 1–9 to select and establish, with later phases overlapping). The pptx five-year draft is not used as the plan. Gates remain subject to programme approval, as the speaker notes say. KGFS’s published enterprise book sits closest to the proposition’s “small” pathway (buyer relationships, receivables, suitable working capital) and its “micro” pathway (cash-flow visibility, documentation). That is a fit of description. It is not a claim that a cohort has been selected.

1. **Name one Tamil Nadu catchment, and describe it as a cohort.** KGFS nominates one rural or high-street nano/micro catchment where it already has branch density and an enterprise-loan book. The public basis for starting in Tamil Nadu is the AUM share on 31 December 2025 (45.8 percent and 46 percent, in the two sentences of the 30 March 2026 CARE note) and the locator concentration queried on 27 November 2026 (161 of 366 listed centres). The nomination itself has to come from KGFS’s own map and from its enterprise-loan team, not from this note. The year’s work is to describe that catchment in the proposition’s operating language: the cohort, the measurable business problem, the receivable pattern, the capability gaps, and the capital pathways, of which KGFS is one. The anchor is whatever committed buyer or anchor corporate actually exists in that catchment. If KGFS cannot name one, that is a gap in the design, and this note does not invent a buyer to fill it. Other lenders remain free to participate. KGFS does not become the cluster’s lender. Business EMI Protection and TReDS onboarding stay Catalyst concepts on the 5 October 2026 proposition. KGFS does not build them.

2. **Offer the suitability and enterprise-readiness method as a preparation tool.** KGFS and, where it agrees, Dvara Research, write down how a household financial well-being assessment (assets, liabilities, income, expenses, goals, and the Plan-Grow-Protect-Diversify sequence) can be used by a Cluster Capability Centre to prepare a nano or micro enterprise before any lender sees a file. FWR 2.0, as FMO describes the grant, is the natural text to draw on if KGFS is willing to share the method rather than the customer records. The output is a preparation standard. The credit decision, including a decision to decline, stays with KGFS or with whichever lender the enterprise chooses. Catalyst does not score the customer and does not tell KGFS the outcome.

3. **Write one co-lending or BC documentation path that is the lender’s own.** KGFS already, on CARE’s account of 30 March 2026, had one NBFC partnership live and two further lenders in advanced talks. The first-year contribution is a plain description of how a cluster enterprise’s documents — identity, cash flow, buyer evidence, capability gaps — can travel into a KGFS credit file or a partner-bank file without Catalyst selecting the partner, setting the price, or seeing the decision. If PERDIX or PERDIX LP is the system a lender chooses to use, that choice belongs to the lender. Solutions can explain the path. Solutions does not become the cluster’s gate.

4. **Ask Dvara Research to mark the cluster scorecard.** This is the brochure’s second founding-support item and its fourth pillar, done as work rather than as a gift: a research view of why segments succeed or fail, and a common outcome scorecard. The scorecard, as the live site publishes it, will track enterprise and cluster readiness, payment friction and receivables health, capability needs, market linkage and buyer access, and capital pathways and documentation gaps. The 5 October 2026 proposition adds baselines and a capability-centre playbook to that evidence system. Research’s job in year one is to say which of those fields a rural or high-street nano enterprise can actually support, which fields would launder a household’s distress into a “readiness” score, and which customer-protection failures (over-indebtedness, consent that is not understood, grievance routes that constrained users cannot use) the scorecard must be able to see in aggregate. Research publishes in its own name, or the platform publishes an aggregated note that Research has cleared. Individual KGFS customer data does not move. Research’s participation is a working arrangement inside the KGFS membership, or a separate research partnership. It is not membership of its own, it is not a seat, and it is not a founding-backer grant unless Research’s board later decides to fund.

5. **Sit in the working group that is designing the framework.** The site offers members “participation in relevant working groups and ecosystem design.” While the public council cards and the Partnerships and Membership role are still unpublished, that design work is what a member contributes. It is not the founding-backer role, which is funding. The group KGFS would actually be useful in is the one that has to make a rural and high-street nano/micro cluster work: how a suitability file, a buyer, a receivable, and more than one lender share a timetable without any of them owning the enterprise. KGFS brings the credit and field practice. Research brings the critique. Solutions brings the partnership-operations view if the lenders want it. Holdings, if it attends, attends as a shareholder and a narrator of the group, not as the party that can bind KGFS.

What this year deliberately does not include: a founding-backer cheque from KGFS, Trust, Research, or Holdings; a multi-state rollout or a commitment to fund 4–5 centres; a Bihar pilot while CARE is flagging the Bihar ordinance as a monitorable (about 12 percent of AUM on 31 December 2025); a Telangana pilot (the FY2024-25 report said the groundwork was being laid; the locator on 27 November 2026 did not list the state); any use of Northern Arc’s balance sheet; any build by KGFS of Business EMI Protection or of TReDS; and any public claim that a council seat or a founding-backer label has been promised.

## 6. What Dvara gets

**Published member benefits, and only these.** The membership page offers four things, plus a sentence underneath: live pilot access (“Participate in real cluster pilots”); partnership access (“Structured routes to collaborate”); readiness and friction intelligence (“Aggregated cluster insight”); credible visibility (“Recognition tied to contribution”); and “participation in relevant working groups and ecosystem design.” Logo display, if KGFS wants it, follows the public rule: active membership, paid, logo consent, not hidden by the secretariat, not expired. The site does not say that any class of member is displayed differently. The brochure’s “what your backing would build” — a secretariat, a research programme, 4–5 Capability Centres, and a shared evidence system — is what a founding backer’s funding would pay for. Those are not a return to a member, and they are not benefits KGFS receives by joining. No document in the documents repository sets a member benefit list beyond the public page.

For KGFS, those published benefits are concrete only in the Tamil Nadu catchment of section 5. Live pilot access means a place in that catchment’s operating rhythm, beside anchors, capability partners, and other lenders, not a right to every future pilot. Partnership access means a structured route to the buyers, the Recovery Cell, and the capability centre, not a joint-venture agreement. Readiness and friction intelligence means the aggregated scorecard, which KGFS can use to see payment friction and capability gaps it does not see from a branch, in a form the site says is de-identified. Visibility means recognition tied to the work in section 5, if the secretariat judges the contribution to be real. The working group is the design table described above.

**Inferences.** These are not published benefits. They are the reasons, on this reading, that the published benefits are worth more to Dvara than a logo.

- The branch network can originate a suitable enterprise loan. It cannot put an anchor, a payment timetable, and a capability partner on the same case. The pilot is the nearest published instrument for that.
- Aggregated friction intelligence is a way to test whether the shift from JLG toward enterprise loans is safer when receivables and capability are handled beside the loan. KGFS cannot generate that evidence by itself without the evidence being read as its own performance report. A neutral scorecard can, if the neutrality rules hold.
- An NBFC member would be early. The agreements’ membership sentence already names non-banking financial companies and micro-finance institutions, and the Sinha letter still says “potentially NBFC.” That is an inference about timing, drawn from those documents. It is not a voting right, and it is not a claim on a council seat. The public About page still shows every seat as vacant. The 5 October 2026 brochure and the Sinha letter already name people on the advisory side and, for three of them, on the governing council. The empty cards are the site lagging the brochure. They are not proof that the council is empty, and they are not an invitation to fill a seat by applying.

Nothing in the published benefits is a cheaper liability, a co-lending mandate, a customer list, or a seat.

## 7. Boundaries and risks

The participation has to be written so that the following stay true in practice.

**No exclusive tie-in.** The Approach page forbids exclusive access and preferred tie-ins. In the Tamil Nadu catchment, KGFS is one capital pathway. Enterprises, anchors, and the Capability Centre remain free to use other lenders. A document that lists KGFS as “the” cluster NBFC would break the model and should not be signed.

**No influence over individual credit decisions.** Catalyst does not underwrite and does not own the lender decision. KGFS does not sit on a Catalyst committee that approves, declines, prices, or waives a loan, whether the loan is KGFS’s or another lender’s. Research’s comments on the scorecard are about the instrument, not about a named borrower. Donor-style language already on the site is the right analogue: funding does not buy influence over individual lending decisions. Membership should not buy it either.

**Catalyst is not a customer-acquisition funnel.** The live site says the platform does not own the customer relationship. The unpublished footer worksheet goes further and says the platform does not allocate leads or route MSMEs to preferred lenders. This participation should be run on the stricter sentence. A KGFS wealth manager may meet an enterprise inside a catchment the enterprise has chosen to enter. The centre does not hand KGFS a list, and KGFS does not fund the centre in exchange for origination. The CRM fee, if any is later set, buys membership. It does not buy files.

**Data and suitability stay with Dvara.** Suitability is KGFS’s conduct obligation, carried out by its wealth managers and credit team, under its board. Customer-level financial well-being reports, account-aggregator data, and credit files stay on KGFS’s systems. Solutions, if it processes a partnership book, does so as the lender’s vendor, under the lender’s instructions. Catalyst’s published use of information is to operate the platform and to publish “only aggregated, de-identified insight.” Research keeps the independence its website states. A research note that uses KGFS operations as fieldwork needs its own consent and its own board, and it is not a condition of membership.

**A council seat is a working role, not a right of membership.** The public page still marks all ten seats “to be announced.” The 5 October 2026 brochure and the Sinha letter have already named people, and this note does not assign the NBFC seat to any of them. The honest seat, if the Foundation later invites a person from the member, is NBFC / Alternative Lender, filled by someone who can speak for KGFS’s credit and field practice and who will do the work. It is not a seat that comes with the membership form. Treating an application as a claim on that seat is the reputational risk. Catalyst would look captured by a lender whose book CARE has just downgraded, and on which asset-quality covenants were breached on 72.1 percent of debt facilities as on 31 December 2025. KGFS would look as if it had sought a public-governance badge during a repair of the JLG book. Neither reading is required by the facts, and both become likely if the seat is discussed as an entitlement.

**The credit position is itself a boundary.** Net stressed assets at 37.42 percent of net worth on 31 December 2025, a loss in the nine months, a BBB rating, and a still-majority JLG book mean that any public association has to be accurate. Membership is not an investment recommendation, not a statement that the enterprise-loan book is clean (CARE says the post-guardrail book’s early-bucket collections were comfortable, and it also says stress will stay elevated), and not a channel for rebuilding AUM. The Bihar exposure and the socio-political risk CARE describes are KGFS’s to manage. They are not Catalyst programmes.

**Other group companies do not ride in on the KGFS form.** Northern Arc’s MSME and LAP book, Solutions’ intention (stated in the impact report) to build “private digital marketplaces for SME clusters,” E-Registry’s farmer work, and Money’s gig-worker work are separate propositions. They are not included in this membership. Solutions’ marketplace sentence is a company plan for FY2025-26 in its own report. It is not a Catalyst workstream.

## 8. Recommendation

The institution which joins as a member is Dvara Kshetriya Gramin Financial Services Private Limited, in the Lenders category on the published form. It holds the rural and nano/micro book, the branches, and the suitability practice. The organisation’s own membership sentence names non-banking financial companies and micro-finance institutions, and the Sinha letter still says “potentially NBFC,” so an NBFC member would be early. That is a membership. It is not a founding-backer subscription.

KGFS should not be the founding backer. On 31 December 2025 it had reported a nine-month loss. CARE downgraded it on 30 March 2026. Asset-quality covenants were breached on 72.1 percent of debt facilities outstanding as on that December date. Funding a Section 8 platform is not a use of that balance sheet. The brochure’s founding-backer role is an individual funding execution, with no financial return and no ticket size stated. If the group wants that conversation, the only honest candidates are a mission entity that actually makes grants. These sources do not show a grant budget. Dvara Trust is the promoter CARE names. Dvara Research is a not-for-profit research institution. The founding-backer door is not recommended for KGFS, and it is not evidenced for Trust or Research. It narrates the FY2024-25 ecosystem report, and it is the shareholder of record in Solutions, Money, Health Finance, Smartgold, Intelligent Systems, and, as an associate, E-Registry. Dvara Research’s role is the brochure’s research programme and the scorecard: a working contribution inside the KGFS membership, or a separate research partnership. It becomes a founding-backer gift only if Research’s board decides to fund, which is not assumed. Dvara Solutions fits the membership sentence’s “fintechs, technology firms” if it ever applied alone, against the Fintech / Digital Infrastructure seat. That would be a different membership, about plumbing, and it would drop the reason this note exists. One application, by KGFS, with a side letter that names Research and Solutions and what they will and will not do, remains the clean structure. Solutions stays a documentation path the lender owns.

The honest council seat, if one is ever offered, is NBFC / Alternative Lender. The public page still says every seat is “to be announced.” The brochure and the Sinha letter have already named people, without saying which of them holds the NBFC seat. The application should not request a seat. A person from KGFS who can speak about suitability, enterprise-loan underwriting, and the limits of a branch is the profile that seat is written for, if the Foundation later offers it. Directors of Digital Growth Infrastructure Foundation can receive an approach. Prashant Mahagaonkar signs the letters used here, and Govind Dichwalkar signs Bonani Roychoudhury’s. The published Partnerships and Membership role is still “Appointment in progress.” An approach does not have to wait for that card to be filled. Filing still waits until four things are true: the KGFS board and Dvara Trust agree that KGFS is the member and that Holdings will not represent the book; KGFS names the Tamil Nadu catchment from its own density and enterprise-loan book, and says whether a committed anchor exists there; a neutrality protocol covers exclusive dealing, lead allocation, customer-level data, and credit decisions, using the stricter worksheet sentence as the working rule; and the application does not request a council seat, a fee waiver, or a founding-backer label. Research’s board should agree the scorecard work before Research is named. Solutions, if named, explains a lender-owned path and does not bring an SME-cluster marketplace into scope. Internally, both sides should keep the 30 March 2026 downgrade, the 37.42 percent net-stressed-assets ratio, and the covenant breaches in view. There is still no published membership fee. The public member benefits remain only those on the membership page. The brochure’s benefits are for funders, and they are not a return.

If those four conditions cannot be agreed, the right outcome is not to file. A generic partnership paragraph, or a cheque from the KGFS balance sheet, would waste the distinctive thing Dvara has to give, which is household suitability, a shifting nano and micro book, and a research institution that can criticise the scorecard.
