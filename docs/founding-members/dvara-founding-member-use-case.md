# Dvara as a founding member of MSME Catalyst

Internal briefing for Dvara’s leadership and for the MSME Catalyst secretariat. It is not a page for msmecatalyst.org. Prepared on 27 November 2026 from the public site, this repository, and the primary Dvara, CARE, FMO, and Northern Arc documents listed in section 10.

No desktop folder of templates was present in the environment used to write this note. The paths checked are listed in section 10. The note therefore follows the language of the live site and this repository. It does not follow an unseen template.

Every material figure below carries the document it comes from and the date that document gives it. Group-level facts and Dvara KGFS facts are kept apart. Where two Dvara documents disagree, both figures are shown.

---

## 1. The ask

Dvara Kshetriya Gramin Financial Services Private Limited (Dvara KGFS) should join MSME Catalyst now, as a lender member, while the Governing Council, the secretariat, and the cluster operating model are still being set. The work of the first year is one Tamil Nadu catchment where KGFS already has branch density and an enterprise-loan book, plus a suitability method, a co-lending file path that remains the lender’s own decision, and a research review of the cluster scorecard. Dvara Research Foundation and Dvara Solutions Private Limited should take part in that work under a written scope. Dvara Holdings should not be the member: its own annual return for the year ended 31 March 2025 does not list KGFS as a subsidiary or an associate, and CARE’s rating note of 30 March 2026 records Holdings at 1.3 percent of KGFS after the May 2025 equity infusion. Northern Arc Capital Limited should not be treated as the Dvara institution that joins. This note does not ask for a fee concession, a logo right, a council seat as an entitlement, or any preferential right to lend.

## 2. MSME Catalyst in its own words

MSME Catalyst is the public name of a platform operated by Digital Growth Infrastructure Foundation, a Section 8 not-for-profit company (CIN U94990MH2026NPL468930), with its registered office at WeWork 247 Park, 13th Floor, Vikhroli Corp, Mumbai 400079. The site describes it as “India’s neutral convergence layer for MSME growth.”

The home page states the problem in one line: “India does not lack MSME support. It lacks convergence.” The same page says the platform “makes existing support work together around real MSME clusters and business journeys” and “brings anchors, MSMEs, lenders, technology providers, capability institutions, legal and dispute-resolution partners and government into one accountable operating model.” The About page states the purpose as making “existing MSME infrastructure usable on the ground — by converging market demand, cash-flow support, capability, capital pathways and intelligence around live clusters.”

The home page names four connected gaps. They are quoted here as published:

| Gap | The site’s heading | The site’s description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Cash-flow gap | Receivables and payment certainty. |
| 2 | Market gap | Demand-side linkage and buyer access. |
| 3 | Capability gap | Technical, managerial, digital and legal handholding. |
| 4 | Growth-capital gap | Preparation, documentation and access pathways. |

The growth-capital gap, in the site’s own words, is preparation, documentation, and access pathways. It is not a pool of money the platform lends.

The home page says support already exists, and that “they often reach the enterprise separately, on different timelines, through different doors.” What the platform says it does is hold a shared cluster operating framework, convene institutions, prepare enterprises and cases, coordinate referrals, track outcomes, and publish aggregated execution insight. The same section says: “MSME Catalyst is the neutral layer that convenes, prepares and tracks. It does not lend, underwrite or adjudicate — independent institutions retain their own eligibility, commercial and legal decisions.”

The hard boundaries, in the site’s language, are these.

- “MSME Catalyst does not own the lender decision, the legal outcome, the customer relationship or the participant’s data.” It “holds the shared operating framework and the aggregated evidence — on behalf of the whole ecosystem, not any one participant.”
- The published footer: “MSME Catalyst does not lend, underwrite, provide legal advice, adjudicate disputes, operate an ODR platform, guarantee recovery, or replace existing schemes, courts, arbitrators, universities or implementation partners.” Lenders, ODR providers, and other institutions “retain their own eligibility, commercial, legal and operational decisions.”
- The Approach page: “MSMEs remain free to choose providers. No participant gets exclusive access or preferred tie-ins. Neutrality is not a slogan here; it is an operating rule.” The home page’s public count is “0 exclusive tie-ins or lock-in.”
- The Cluster Capability Centre “is not a lender, college, ODR platform or legal service.” Its finance function is “Readiness and documentation — not lending decisions, which stay with lenders.” Its legal function is “Case preparation and neutral referral — not legal advice or adjudication.”
- ODR support organises documents and refers to an independent provider. The platform “is not an ODR platform, law firm, mediator, arbitrator or court,” and it “does not provide legal advice, compel participation, collect money or guarantee recovery.” The statutory MSEFC route “remains available and is not replaced.”
- Terms of use: a referral “is neutral. MSMEs retain choice of provider. Providers retain all their own decisions.”
- Donor funding “does not buy influence over individual lending decisions, ODR referrals, policy positions or case outcomes.”

The operating method is published as four steps: start with demand (anchors, buyers, products, supply chains, markets); build a Cluster Capability Centre; use shared scorecards of readiness, payment friction, capability needs, market linkage, and capital pathways; protect neutrality. Four programme pillars are named: Awareness and Readiness (including Credit Ki Kaksha, Rating Ki Kaksha, and Digital Dost); Receivables and ODR Support (including a Cluster Recovery Cell for case preparation); Capital and Market Pathways; and Cluster Intelligence. The Reports, Blogs, and Events pages are still CMS placeholders (“title to be added”). No live pilot cluster is named on the public site.

Membership, in the site’s words, “is a working role inside cluster infrastructure” and “is institutional, invitation-led and outcome-oriented — not generic logo placement or event access.” It is for banks, NBFCs, and regulated financial institutions; fintechs, TSPs, LSPs, and data and infrastructure providers; rating and ecosystem institutions; anchor corporates and market-access organisations; and funders. The application form uses the categories Lenders, Fintechs, Infrastructure, Anchors, Ecosystem Institutions, ODR Providers, and Donors and Funding Partners. Submissions “are reviewed by the Secretariat and managed in the membership CRM.” The public member wall is generated only from records that are active, paid, and covered by logo consent. The CRM schema in this repository has a fee field. The public site does not publish a fee.

The Governing Council is ten seats. Each card on the About page still reads “Member to be announced.” The seats include Banking or Financial Institution Member, NBFC / Alternative Lender Member, and Fintech / Digital Infrastructure Member, along with anchor, MSME, capability, legal and receivables, and three independent seats. Secretariat roles, including Partnerships and Membership, are published as “Appointment in progress.”

**What “founding member” means in this note.** The public site does not publish a founding-member product, fee, or benefit list. This repository does not define one. `CONTENT-TO-FILL.md` leaves the council names blank and does not define founding membership. Founding membership, as used here, means joining while the council, the secretariat appointments, and the operating model are still being set, so the institution helps design the shared cluster framework rather than only consuming a finished one. That is a statement about timing and about the work. It is not a grant of a council seat, a logo privilege beyond the ordinary consent rules, a fee waiver, or a preferential lending right. None of those is published, and this note does not create them.

A stricter sentence sits in `CONTENT-TO-FILL.md` as unpublished worksheet text for the footer: the platform “does not … allocate leads, route MSMEs to preferred lenders.” The live footer does not yet say that. This note treats the live site as the public rule, and treats the worksheet line as the safer operating rule for any Dvara participation, because it is already the direction of the organisation’s own draft.

## 3. Dvara as it is today

Dvara is a group of institutions around one published mission. It is not one lender, and it is not one cap table.

### Mission

Dvara Holdings’ website states: “Our mission is to ensure that every individual and every enterprise has complete access to financial services.” Dvara KGFS’s website uses the same mission for the trust that incorporated it, and then states KGFS’s own mission as maximising “the financial well-being of every individual and every enterprise by providing complete access to financial services in rural India.” Dvara Research’s website states a related and narrower mission: every low-income household and every small enterprise should have “complete access to suitable financial services and social security.”

The word that matters for this note is “suitable.” KGFS’s company-overview page, which is undated and which still carries an older branch count (see below), describes a “Wealth Management” approach in which “a financial well-being report, which serves as a tool to assess the suitability of a customer’s chosen product, is created by gathering information on a household’s assets, liabilities, income, expenses, and financial goals.” The product-philosophy page organises that approach as Plan, Grow, Protect, and Diversify. FMO’s MASSIF note on 2025 capacity-development contracts describes a grant to update that tool: a Business Intelligence platform, a Customer App, and “the updated Financial Wellbeing Report (FWR 2.0),” in support of a shift “from group-loan-dominated lending to a more diverse individual and MSME portfolio.”

## 4. Why the fit is specific

The weak argument is that Dvara lends to small firms. Many NBFCs do. The argument that is specific to these two institutions is that an enterprise in a cluster fails when demand, receivables, capability, and capital arrive separately, and that Dvara’s own model already refuses to treat the loan as the whole of the household’s problem.

KGFS enrols a household, builds a financial well-being picture from assets, liabilities, income, expenses, and goals, and uses that picture to test suitability. The loan that is growing inside the book is a livelihood or enterprise loan, cash-flow based, aimed at nano and micro-enterprises in rural and high-street markets, not only a group loan for consumption smoothing. Around that loan, the group has spent years on the institutional layer: Research on suitable finance and customer protection, Solutions on the plumbing of co-lending and partnership books, and a branch architecture that KGFS itself calls a dense cluster. MSME Catalyst’s published belief is the same shape of problem from the other side. The enterprise should not have to stitch the doors together. The platform will hold the frame and the evidence, and will not own the credit decision.

Mapped onto the four gaps, the assets are these.

**Cash-flow and receivables.** KGFS already looks at household cash flow, including through account aggregators, and it lives with the consequences when that cash flow breaks: the JLG stress, the guardrails, the ARC sale, the rating. What a branch cannot convene is the buyer. Catalyst’s published cash-flow work is payment certainty, a Cluster Recovery Cell that prepares invoices, purchase orders, and delivery proof, and a neutral referral to an independent ODR provider, without replacing MSEFC and without guaranteeing recovery. For a high-street or village enterprise, the missing piece beside a KGFS enterprise loan is often the receivable from a buyer the branch does not sit with. That is the gap Catalyst is built to hold. KGFS would meet it as one lender in a cluster, with its own collections and its own duty on suitability, while the buyer-side friction is prepared for whatever route the enterprise chooses.

**Market.** Catalyst says it starts with demand: anchors, buyers, products, and supply chains. KGFS’s enterprise loans follow enterprises that are already trading — the impact report’s high-street nano and micro-enterprises, and the customer stories of shops, dairy, and village trade in Tamil Nadu, Odisha, Karnataka, and Chhattisgarh. Those stories show the customer type. They do not show a buyer programme. Catalyst’s market gap is the part of the model a rural NBFC does not own: the anchor at the other end of the order. A founding role would put KGFS’s existing density next to that demand work, in a catchment KGFS already serves, rather than asking KGFS to invent a new geography.

**Capability.** Catalyst’s third gap is technical, managerial, digital, and legal handholding, through a centre that coordinates and does not replace a college or a lender. KGFS’s published capability is different and useful: wealth managers who build a household plan, digital agents, a financial well-being report, and, on the research side, a body of work on how low-income households actually manage money and how customer protection fails. Research’s current programme includes household money management, consent in digital lending, and monitoring of customer protection in microfinance. That is a method for enterprise readiness. It is not a substitute for the handholding Catalyst says a cluster still needs, and it is not a licence for Research to underwrite.

**Growth capital.** Catalyst defines this gap as preparation, documentation, and access pathways. KGFS is one such pathway, for a ticket and a geography — rural and high-street nano and micro enterprises, still majority-unsecured, with a secured share of about 2 percent — that a typical commercial-bank MSME desk and a typical digital personal-loan fintech do not both occupy. Solutions’ co-lending middleware, and KGFS’s own move into BC and co-lending with one unnamed NBFC already live as of the 30 March 2026 note, is a way for a second balance sheet to participate without Catalyst choosing it. The credit decision, the price, and the suitability duty stay with the regulated lender that actually books the loan.

**What Catalyst gains that it cannot get from a generic NBFC or a generic fintech.**

A generic NBFC can offer a credit appetite and a logo. A generic fintech can offer a journey and a webhook. Neither, on the public record, brings all of the following at once.

- An operating suitability practice for rural households, already described by KGFS as a financial well-being report and already being rebuilt, on FMO’s account, as FWR 2.0, aimed at individual and MSME lending rather than at a group-loan monoculture.
- Branch density in the places the enterprises actually are. Tamil Nadu alone was about 46 percent of KGFS AUM on 31 December 2025, and the public locator, whatever its gap against the CARE branch count, lists 161 Tamil Nadu centres. The other ten states are real and smaller. A generic lender that wants a metro MSME book does not bring this map.
- A research institution that is legally outside the lender, states its independence as the condition of speaking for the low-income household, and has a published mission of suitable finance. Catalyst’s scorecard — readiness, payment friction, capability, market linkage, capital pathways — needs that kind of criticism while the scorecard is still being designed. A lender marking its own homework does not provide it. Research can, only if its independence is written down and kept.
- Technology that other lenders already use for partnership books. If the cluster needs a co-lending or BC file, Solutions is a vendor with a stated co-lending product, not a reason for Catalyst to build or own one. That keeps the platform out of the lender’s system, which is what the site says it wants.
- A group that has already described the last two years as a test of whether inclusion survives stress, and that has answered by cutting JLG growth and putting more of the book into livelihood and enterprise loans. Catalyst’s thesis is that capital without the other three gaps is how enterprises fail. KGFS is living the credit half of that sentence in public, including the losses and the downgrade. A founding member that has already had that experience is more useful to the design of the framework than a member that arrives only to distribute loans into a finished cluster.

**What KGFS cannot convene alone, which is what membership is for.** A wealth manager in a 25-kilometre branch radius can see the household. The wealth manager cannot require a buyer to pay on time, cannot be the neutral party that prepares a receivable for ODR, cannot be the academic or skilling partner, and cannot publish de-identified cluster evidence without being read as marketing the book. Those are the jobs the site assigns to the platform. They are also the jobs that make an enterprise loan safer to originate, which is in KGFS’s interest and is still not a right to the customer.

The fit has a boundary inside it. Catalyst’s model starts with demand and with anchors. KGFS’s model starts with the household and the village branch. The first-year design has to hold both, or the cluster will become either a lending camp or a buyer event with no credit path. Section 5 is written to hold both.

## 5. What Dvara would do in the first year

Five contributions. Each one stays inside Catalyst’s neutrality. None of them is a right to a council seat, a right to exclusive origination, or a transfer of customer data. The catchment is Tamil Nadu because that is the state the sources support as KGFS’s largest geography. This note does not name a district cluster, a product cluster, or a city. Customer stories on the KGFS website are set in places such as Sivakasi, Villupuram, and Tirunelveli. A story is not a density study. No source opened here supports a Dvara role in a Pune auto-component cluster, and none is proposed.

1. **Name one Tamil Nadu catchment, and put it into the cluster frame.** KGFS nominates one rural or high-street nano/micro catchment where it already has branch density and an enterprise-loan book. The public basis for starting in Tamil Nadu is the AUM share on 31 December 2025 (45.8 percent and 46 percent, in the two sentences of the 30 March 2026 CARE note) and the locator concentration queried on 27 November 2026 (161 of 366 listed centres). The nomination itself has to come from KGFS’s own map and from its enterprise-loan team, not from this note. The year’s work is to describe that catchment in Catalyst’s operating language: the buyers and anchors, if any; the receivable pattern; the capability gaps; and the capital pathways, of which KGFS is one. Other lenders remain free to participate. KGFS does not become the cluster’s lender.

2. **Offer the suitability and enterprise-readiness method as a preparation tool.** KGFS and, where it agrees, Dvara Research, write down how a household financial well-being assessment (assets, liabilities, income, expenses, goals, and the Plan-Grow-Protect-Diversify sequence) can be used by a Cluster Capability Centre to prepare a nano or micro enterprise before any lender sees a file. FWR 2.0, as FMO describes the grant, is the natural text to draw on if KGFS is willing to share the method rather than the customer records. The output is a preparation standard. The credit decision, including a decision to decline, stays with KGFS or with whichever lender the enterprise chooses. Catalyst does not score the customer and does not tell KGFS the outcome.

3. **Write one co-lending or BC documentation path that is the lender’s own.** KGFS already, on CARE’s account of 30 March 2026, had one NBFC partnership live and two further lenders in advanced talks. The first-year contribution is a plain description of how a cluster enterprise’s documents — identity, cash flow, buyer evidence, capability gaps — can travel into a KGFS credit file or a partner-bank file without Catalyst selecting the partner, setting the price, or seeing the decision. If PERDIX or PERDIX LP is the system a lender chooses to use, that choice belongs to the lender. Solutions can explain the path. Solutions does not become the cluster’s gate.

4. **Ask Dvara Research to mark the cluster scorecard.** The scorecard, as published, will track enterprise and cluster readiness, payment friction and receivables health, capability needs, market linkage and buyer access, and capital pathways and documentation gaps. Research’s job in year one is to say which of those fields a rural or high-street nano enterprise can actually support, which fields would launder a household’s distress into a “readiness” score, and which customer-protection failures (over-indebtedness, consent that is not understood, grievance routes that constrained users cannot use) the scorecard must be able to see in aggregate. Research publishes in its own name, or the platform publishes an aggregated note that Research has cleared. Individual KGFS customer data does not move. Research’s participation is a working arrangement. It is not membership, and it is not a seat.

5. **Sit in the working group that is designing the framework.** The site offers members “participation in relevant working groups and ecosystem design.” While the council seats and the secretariat are still unpublished names, that design work is the founding role. The group Dvara would actually be useful in is the one that has to make a rural and high-street nano/micro cluster work: how a suitability file, a buyer, a receivable, and more than one lender share a timetable without any of them owning the enterprise. KGFS brings the credit and field practice. Research brings the critique. Solutions brings the partnership-operations view if the lenders want it. Holdings, if it attends, attends as a shareholder and a narrator of the group, not as the party that can bind KGFS.

What this year deliberately does not include: a multi-state rollout, a Bihar pilot while CARE is flagging the Bihar ordinance as a monitorable (about 12 percent of AUM on 31 December 2025), a Telangana pilot (the FY2024-25 report said the groundwork was being laid; the locator on 27 November 2026 did not list the state), any use of Northern Arc’s balance sheet, and any public claim that a council seat has been promised.

## 6. What Dvara gets

**Published member benefits, and only these.** The membership page offers four things, plus a sentence underneath: live pilot access (“Participate in real cluster pilots”); partnership access (“Structured routes to collaborate”); readiness and friction intelligence (“Aggregated cluster insight”); credible visibility (“Recognition tied to contribution”); and “participation in relevant working groups and ecosystem design.” Logo display, if Dvara wants it, follows the public rule: active membership, paid, logo consent, not hidden by the secretariat, not expired. The site does not say that a founding member is displayed differently from any other member.

For KGFS, those published benefits are concrete only in the Tamil Nadu catchment of section 5. Live pilot access means a place in that catchment’s operating rhythm, beside anchors, capability partners, and other lenders, not a right to every future pilot. Partnership access means a structured route to the buyers, the Recovery Cell, and the capability centre, not a joint-venture agreement. Readiness and friction intelligence means the aggregated scorecard, which KGFS can use to see payment friction and capability gaps it does not see from a branch, in a form the site says is de-identified. Visibility means recognition tied to the work in section 5, if the secretariat judges the contribution to be real. The working group is the design table described above.

**Inferences.** These are not published benefits. They are the reasons, on this reading, that the published benefits are worth more to Dvara than a logo.

- The branch network can originate a suitable enterprise loan. It cannot put an anchor, a payment timetable, and a capability partner on the same case. The pilot is the nearest published instrument for that.
- Aggregated friction intelligence is a way to test whether the shift from JLG toward enterprise loans is safer when receivables and capability are handled beside the loan. KGFS cannot generate that evidence by itself without the evidence being read as its own performance report. A neutral scorecard can, if the neutrality rules hold.
- Joining while the council, the secretariat, and the scorecard are unfinished is the only moment at which a rural suitability practice can shape the framework. Waiting for a finished cluster means arriving as one more lender on a pathway designed around someone else’s book. That timing advantage is an inference from the empty council cards and the placeholder programmes. It is not a voting right.

Nothing in the published benefits is a cheaper liability, a co-lending mandate, a customer list, or a seat.

## 7. Boundaries and risks

The participation has to be written so that the following stay true in practice.

**No exclusive tie-in.** The Approach page forbids exclusive access and preferred tie-ins. In the Tamil Nadu catchment, KGFS is one capital pathway. Enterprises, anchors, and the Capability Centre remain free to use other lenders. A document that lists KGFS as “the” cluster NBFC would break the model and should not be signed.

**No influence over individual credit decisions.** Catalyst does not underwrite and does not own the lender decision. KGFS does not sit on a Catalyst committee that approves, declines, prices, or waives a loan, whether the loan is KGFS’s or another lender’s. Research’s comments on the scorecard are about the instrument, not about a named borrower. Donor-style language already on the site is the right analogue: funding does not buy influence over individual lending decisions. Membership should not buy it either.

**Catalyst is not a customer-acquisition funnel.** The live site says the platform does not own the customer relationship. The unpublished footer worksheet goes further and says the platform does not allocate leads or route MSMEs to preferred lenders. This participation should be run on the stricter sentence. A KGFS wealth manager may meet an enterprise inside a catchment the enterprise has chosen to enter. The centre does not hand KGFS a list, and KGFS does not fund the centre in exchange for origination. The CRM fee, if any is later set, buys membership. It does not buy files.

**Data and suitability stay with Dvara.** Suitability is KGFS’s conduct obligation, carried out by its wealth managers and credit team, under its board. Customer-level financial well-being reports, account-aggregator data, and credit files stay on KGFS’s systems. Solutions, if it processes a partnership book, does so as the lender’s vendor, under the lender’s instructions. Catalyst’s published use of information is to operate the platform and to publish “only aggregated, de-identified insight.” Research keeps the independence its website states. A research note that uses KGFS operations as fieldwork needs its own consent and its own board, and it is not a condition of membership.

**A council seat is a working role, not a right of membership.** All ten seats are “to be announced.” The honest seat, if the council later invites a person, is NBFC / Alternative Lender, filled by someone who can speak for KGFS’s credit and field practice and who will do the work. It is not a seat that comes with the membership form. Treating an application as a claim on that seat is the reputational risk. Catalyst would look captured by a lender whose book CARE has just downgraded, and on which asset-quality covenants were breached on 72.1 percent of debt facilities as on 31 December 2025. KGFS would look as if it had sought a public-governance badge during a repair of the JLG book. Neither reading is required by the facts, and both become likely if the seat is discussed as an entitlement.

**The credit position is itself a boundary.** Net stressed assets at 37.42 percent of net worth on 31 December 2025, a loss in the nine months, a BBB rating, and a still-majority JLG book mean that any public association has to be accurate. Membership is not an investment recommendation, not a statement that the enterprise-loan book is clean (CARE says the post-guardrail book’s early-bucket collections were comfortable, and it also says stress will stay elevated), and not a channel for rebuilding AUM. The Bihar exposure and the socio-political risk CARE describes are KGFS’s to manage. They are not Catalyst programmes.

**Other group companies do not ride in on the KGFS form.** Northern Arc’s MSME and LAP book, Solutions’ intention (stated in the impact report) to build “private digital marketplaces for SME clusters,” E-Registry’s farmer work, and Money’s gig-worker work are separate propositions. They are not included in this membership. Solutions’ marketplace sentence is a company plan for FY2025-26 in its own report. It is not a Catalyst workstream.

## 8. Recommendation

The member should be Dvara Kshetriya Gramin Financial Services Private Limited, in the Lenders category on the published form. It is the regulated entity that holds the rural and nano/micro book, the branches, the suitability practice, and the enterprise-loan shift. The contact route the site actually publishes is membership@msmecatalyst.org, and the form is reviewed by a secretariat whose incumbents are still “Appointment in progress.” An application should wait until the points below are decided, and until there is a named person on the Catalyst side to receive it. Sending the form into an empty secretariat is how a careful proposal becomes a CRM row.

Dvara Holdings should not be the member. It is the right narrator of the FY2024-25 ecosystem report, and it is the shareholder of record in Solutions, Money, Health Finance, Smartgold, Intelligent Systems, and, as an associate, E-Registry. It holds 1.3 percent of KGFS on the post-infusion cap table CARE published on 30 March 2026. It cannot commit KGFS’s capital, customers, or staff. If the group wants a single convening signature, that is a question for the KGFS board and for Dvara Trust, which CARE calls the founding promoter and which holds 28.3 percent. It is not a reason to put Holdings on the form in KGFS’s place.

Dvara Research Foundation and Dvara Solutions should be working participants, not substitute members. Research is outside Holdings’ subsidiary list, promoter-held by a trust, and useful only while it can disagree with the lender. Solutions is a 21.79 percent associate and a vendor to many institutions; the honest category if it ever applied on its own would be Fintechs or Infrastructure, against the Fintech / Digital Infrastructure council seat. That would be a different membership, about plumbing. It would drop the reason this note exists. One application, by KGFS, with a side letter that names Research and Solutions and what they will and will not do, is the clean structure.

The honest council seat, if one is ever offered, is NBFC / Alternative Lender. It is not offered by the site today. It should not be requested in the application. A person from KGFS who can speak about suitability, enterprise-loan underwriting, and the limits of a branch is the profile that seat is written for. A Holdings director who does not run the book, or a Solutions executive, would be the wrong occupant of that seat. The Fintech / Digital Infrastructure seat is a different conversation, and only if Solutions applies for itself.

Before anyone sends an application, six things should be decided in writing.

1. The KGFS board, and Dvara Trust as the promoter CARE describes, agree that KGFS is the member and that Holdings will not represent the book.
2. KGFS names the Tamil Nadu catchment from its own branch density and enterprise-loan book, and states what “density” means in branches and in outstanding enterprise loans. This note cannot name it.
3. A neutrality protocol covers exclusive dealing, lead allocation, customer-level data, and credit decisions, using the stricter worksheet sentence as the working rule.
4. Research’s board agrees the scorecard review, and agrees that the review does not compromise the independence the institution publishes.
5. Solutions’ role, if any, is limited to explaining a lender-owned documentation path. No SME-cluster marketplace is in scope.
6. The application does not ask for a council seat, a fee that has not been published, or a statement about asset quality beyond what CARE has already published. Internally, KGFS and Catalyst should both acknowledge the 30 March 2026 downgrade, the 37.42 percent net-stressed-assets ratio, and the covenant breaches, so that neither side discovers them later as a surprise.

If those six points cannot be agreed, the right outcome is not to file. A generic partnership paragraph would waste the only distinctive thing Dvara has to give, which is the combination of household suitability, a shifting nano and micro book, and a research institution that can criticise the scorecard.
