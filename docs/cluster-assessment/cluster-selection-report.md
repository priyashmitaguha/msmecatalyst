## Section 1 — Identified clusters, methodology and data

### 1.1 Assessment universe

The brief asks for industries or clusters in a **new wave**: a public funding, licensing or investment regime that created a new population of enterprises, licences or investment commitments, with a micro or small population a Capability Centre could actually serve.

Inclusion rule used here:

- A central policy, licensing order or incentive **notified in or after 2018**, and
- A population of manufacturers, licensees or micro enterprises that is not only the list of incentive winners.

Nine entries meet that rule and are profiled in full. Four further waves were screened and are **not scored**, because the observable population is a short list of large projects (section 1.7). Older MSME belts — seafood processing, apparel, leather, gems, general engineering job-work — are a real second universe. They are outside this vintage rule and are not given charter scores here.

The nine profiled entries:

1. Auto components in the electric-vehicle and advanced-automotive-technology wave.
2. Medical devices after the device licensing regime and the device PLI.
3. Technical textiles after the National Technical Textiles Mission.
4. Toys after the quality-control and tariff shift.
5. Defence indigenisation suppliers (licences, positive lists, iDEX).
6. Micro food-processing enterprises in the PMFME formalisation wave.
7. Electronics, scored on the **component and non-PLI supplier tier**, not on the mobile PLI winners.
8. Solar PV module manufacturing after ALMM and the solar PLI.
9. Civil drones after the Drone Rules, 2021 and the drone PLI.

An entry is an industry wave with a **proposed cohort** a centre would serve. National totals are the frame. They are not the catchment of one building.

### 1.2 How to read a number

Three labels are used throughout. They are not interchangeable.

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Official | A ministry, regulator or statistics office published the figure, for a stated period and population. |
| Association or administrator | An industry body or scheme administrator published it. It may be the best available industry total. It is not a statistical census. |
| Not observed | No source in this review publishes the figure. Nothing was imputed. |

Hard separations, applied in every profile:

- A PLI **investment commitment** or **cumulative scheme sales** figure is not gross turnover of the industry, and it is not MSME turnover.
- An association **membership**, a **BIS licence**, a **CDSCO manufacturing licence**, an **ALMM enlistment** and a **Udyam registration** are different populations. A firm can be in one and not the others. A licence is sometimes per factory and per product class, so licence counts exceed enterprise counts.
- An **export value** is not an export-enterprise count. An **export authorisation** is not an exporter. An IEC holder is not a firm that exported that heading in FY2024–25.
- **GVA is not turnover.** Factory **output** is not turnover. **Production** estimates and **customs exports** answer different questions.
- **Enterprise share is not turnover share.** ACMA’s statement that its members account for more than 90 per cent of organised turnover is a turnover concentration statement, not a count of firms above ₹500 crore.
- Where two official sentences disagree (defence production ₹1,50,590 crore against a later “₹1.54 lakh crore”; electronics production ₹11.3 lakh crore against a later “~12 lakh crore”), both are shown and the profile uses the more precise release. Neither is averaged.

### 1.3 Turnover bands — method and why most cells are blank

The bands requested are: above ₹500 crore; ₹200–500 crore; ₹100–200 crore; ₹10–100 crore; up to ₹10 crore; plus an “export-ready” count inside the three upper bands.

No public source used in this review — MoSPI’s Annual Survey of Industries, the Annual Survey of Unincorporated Sector Enterprises, Udyam dashboards as consulted, DGCIS commodity releases, PLI award lists, CDSCO, DGCA, MNRE ALMM, or the association releases — publishes enterprise counts on these rupee cuts for these industries.

What was deliberately **not** done:

- Udyam micro / small / medium classes were **not** mapped onto these bands. From 1 April 2025 the MSME ceilings are turnover up to ₹10 crore (micro), ₹100 crore (small) and ₹500 crore (medium), but classification is investment **and** turnover. A Udyam “small” enterprise is not “a firm with turnover of ₹10–100 crore”. This review did not pull a NIC-level Udyam extract, so even the official class counts are not filled in.
- Listed-company revenues were **not** tallied into a “number of firms above ₹500 crore”. That tally would be a lower bound of firms with a public annual report, not a census, and it was not compiled.
- PLI applicant lists were **not** treated as the above-₹500-crore population. Several drone PLI beneficiaries had a combined turnover of ₹319 crore in FY2021–22. Incentive winners can be small.
- “Export-ready” was **not** inferred from turnover. Export-ready, for this report, would mean a firm that has exported the relevant heading, or that holds the licence a buyer requires (BIS toy licence, CDSCO device licence, ALMM, defence export authorisation). Those lists are not matched to turnover.

**Band result for every profiled entry:** counts in fields 10–17 are **not observed**. Field 22 in each profile points back to this section. The first 90 days of any centre should build an opt-in cohort file. It should not backfill these cells from proportions.

The only size information that *is* usable is qualitative and is stated as such: drone PLI eligibility was turnover of ₹2 crore for drone makers and ₹50 lakh for component makers, and the 23 shortlisted firms together reported ₹319 crore of sales in FY2021–22. That is evidence the drone manufacturing wave was, at that date, a small-firm population. It is not a current band census.

### 1.4 Domestic sales versus exports

A split is stated only when both parts come from the **same** source and the source says they belong to the same total. Ratios of a ministry production estimate to a customs export figure are labelled as ratios of two different series, not as a turnover split.

ACMA’s FY2024–25 release does **not** reconcile. Turnover is ₹6.73 lakh crore. Separately it states domestic OEM supplies of ₹5.70 lakh crore, exports of ₹1,92,346 crore and aftermarket of ₹99,948 crore. Those three lines add to about ₹8.62 lakh crore, not ₹6.73 lakh crore. This report does **not** compute an export share by subtraction. The lines are quoted as published. The association’s note says turnover includes OEM supplies, aftermarket and exports, including unorganised and captive supply, and excludes tyres, batteries, paints and consumables. The arithmetic gap is a limitation of the published review, not a number to be forced.

### 1.5 Finance, insurance and capital — shared facts

These routes exist nationally. They are not evidence that a given cluster uses them.

**Microenterprise debt routes**

- NBFC-MFIs are a Reserve Bank category. Their qualifying assets are small, short-tenor loans to low-income borrowers. That route can serve a household or SHG-scale food unit. It does not serve a factory at ₹10–100 crore.
- NABARD’s SHG–bank linkage, and PMFME seed capital to SHG members, are active in micro food processing. PMFME is a credit-linked subsidy: the bank still sanctions the loan.
- NABARD and the Department of Fisheries’ PMMSY / FIDF are the relevant public routes for fishing and aquaculture households. They are not a working-capital line for an auto-component firm.
- MUDRA (Shishu, Kishore, Tarun) and PMEGP reach micro enterprises. CGTMSE is a **guarantee**, not a loan and not an insurance policy.
- TReDS is a receivable-discounting route for MSME suppliers of large buyers. It is relevant in auto components, electronics and, where the buyer is a large hospital chain or distributor, medical devices. It is a weak fit for a kirana-facing food unit.

**Sector programmes named because a ministry published them**

- PM E-DRIVE, ₹10,900 crore, 1 October 2024 to 31 March 2026, demand incentives for specified electric vehicles. The beneficiary of the incentive is the buyer; OEMs are registered for reimbursement. Not a loan to a component MSME.
- FAME-II reimbursements to OEMs of ₹6,559 crore over the scheme, which ended 31 March 2024. Official.
- PLI Auto, budgetary outlay ₹25,938 crore. 82 approved applicants as on 30 November 2025. Cumulative determined sales under the scheme ₹32,879 crore till 30 September 2025. This is scheme sales of advanced-technology products, not ACMA industry turnover.
- PLI medical devices, outlay ₹3,420 crore. 28 applicants approved; incentive of ₹157.15 crore disbursed to seven applicants till December 2025. Cumulative eligible sales under the scheme ₹13,624.52 crore, of which exports ₹6,425.48 crore, till December 2025. Scheme sales, not industry turnover.
- PMFME, outlay ₹10,000 crore, credit-linked subsidy. 1,72,707 micro enterprises approved for assistance and 59,202 formalised, both as on 31 December 2025. These two official counts are different events. Seed capital to 3,76,326 SHG members, ₹1,282.99 crore.
- PLI for food processing: MoFPI/PIB reports investment of ₹9,032 crore, production/sales of ₹3,80,350 crore and employment of 3,40,116 (direct and indirect), with 70 MSMEs directly enrolled and 40 as contract manufacturers. The sales figure is beneficiary sales under the scheme, dominated by firms large enough to meet PLI thresholds. It is not microenterprise turnover.
- NTTM, launched 2020, outlay ₹1,480 crore, extended to 31 March 2026. GREAT startup grant up to ₹50 lakh; 24 startups approved. PLI textiles: 30 participant companies had commenced production, as stated in the Ministry of Textiles review consulted.
- Drone PLI, notified 30 September 2021, outlay ₹120 crore, performance years FY2021–22 to FY2023–24. A parliamentary reply reported about ₹98.32 crore disbursed. The scheme window is closed.
- iDEX grants up to ₹1.5 crore to startups and MSMEs. That is a grant, not a loan and not venture capital. ADITI is a separate, larger grant window for strategic technologies. ₹449.62 crore was allocated to iDEX including ADITI for 2025–26.

**Insurance**

- ECGC export-credit insurance is the relevant public cover for a firm that actually exports. It does not cover domestic receivables.
- Marine cargo is a commercial policy used on export shipments, including seafood and engineered goods.
- Product liability is commercially available and is the cover that matters for medical devices and toys. No official statistic of how many of these enterprises hold it was found.
- Standard fire, machinery breakdown and related industrial policies are commercial. They are not MSME-specific.
- Crop insurance (PMFBY) insures the farmer, not the food processor.
- No cluster-specific microinsurance product was verified for auto components, medical devices, electronics, solar, toys, technical textiles or drones. Absence from this desk review is not proof a product does not exist.

**Venture and private equity**

- Anchor and listed capital is visible in EVs, electronics contract manufacturing, solar module makers and a small number of drone and defence firms (including the ideaForge listing). That capital does not pass through to a Tier-3 machinist, a BIS-licensed toy unit, or a PMFME enterprise.
- The 24 NTTM GREAT startups and the iDEX grant cohort are **grant** recipients. Counting them as VC-funded enterprises would be a category error.
- No public series counts PE/VC deals into enterprises below ₹100 crore in these waves. The cell is “not observed”, with the qualitative split above.

### 1.6 Comparison

Cluster-shape, 1 to 5, is the average judgement of four observable features: depth of the micro and small population, geographic concentration, visible buyer demand, and whether a physical or hub-and-spoke centre could reach that population. It is **not** in the charter formula.

## Section 2 — Charter alignment

### 2.1 What is being scored

MSME Catalyst’s charter is a convergence layer. It prepares, refers and measures. It does not lend, underwrite, adjudicate, allocate leads or operate an ODR platform. The 16 tools are the work a Capability Centre is allowed to do.

A high industry-investment story can score badly here. Solar module manufacturing is the clearest case: large public incentives, concentrated plants, and almost no work for credit literacy, SHG finance or a microenterprise recovery cell. Micro food processing is the opposite case: a deep micro population and a live credit scheme, but weak buyer-payment friction and no single place to put a building. Cluster-shape (Section 1.6) captures the building question. The charter score captures the 16 tools. They are reported separately so that a high shape cannot launder a weak tool fit, and a high tool average cannot launder a population that is spread across 726 districts.

Additionality, in the formula, means: do these tools add something that the park, the scheme administrator and the anchor do not already do? It does not mean “is the industry under-invested?”. Auto components score high on tool fit and only then clear the line on additionality, because OEM vendor development and common facilities do not run a receivables-to-credit file. They would score lower if the proposal were another tool room.

### 2.2 The 16 tools and the formula

Pillar 1 — Awareness and readiness: Credit Ki Kaksha; Rating Ki Kaksha; Digital Dost; Policy and programme readiness.

Pillar 2 — Faster receivables: cluster-led payment support; ODR recovery pathway; Business EMI protection (an early-stage concept, not a product); recovery and repayment outcomes.

Pillar 3 — Capital and market access: capital pathways; scheme usability; lender pathways; market access.

Pillar 4 — Research, mapping and scorecards: cluster mapping; readiness and friction research; scorecards and dashboards; compliance intelligence.

A tool is **Activate** at 4 or 5, **Later** at 3, and **Skip** at 1 or 2. A pillar score is the arithmetic mean of its four tools. It is not re-weighted inside the pillar.

Overall = P1 × 18% + P2 × 24% + P3 × 18% + P4 × 14% + delivery fit × 16% + additionality × 10%.

| Overall score | Tier |
|---|---|
| 4.00 and above | High |
| 3.00 to below 4.00 | Medium |
| 2.00 to below 3.00 | Watch |
| Below 2.00 | Out of charter |

Delivery fit is whether a physical or hub-and-spoke centre can reach the cohort in one geography with the staff a centre actually has. Additionality is defined in §2.1.

Business EMI protection is scored as a **need to explore**, never as availability. No entry is told that the product exists.

### 2.5 Why the scores sit where they do

**Auto components, 4.000, High.** The business problem is a Tier-2 or Tier-3 firm whose books, e-invoice trail and receivable ageing are not in a state a bank or a TReDS platform will accept, selling to an OEM system that already pays on a long cycle. Evidence is the structure of the industry ACMA describes (OEM supplies, a large aftermarket, and an explicit inclusion of smaller and unorganised players in the turnover), the PLI domestic-value-addition rule that is now pulling parts into localisation, and the existence of TReDS and MSEFC as routes the small firm does not complete. Proposed intervention: Credit Ki Kaksha and a payment register for firms that opt in, then a lender or TReDS introduction only after the file exists. Market access is Later, not Activate: ACMA and the OEMs already run vendor development, and a centre that starts a parallel buyer fair will duplicate them. Compliance is Later: QCO and AIS matters are real but are not the binding gap relative to cash. Business EMI protection is Later: schedule cuts do create EMI stress, and the concept should be tested with lenders, not offered. Additionality is 4 because the common facility and the OEM do not run this file. It would fall to 2 if the proposal were a new tool room. Delivery is 4, not 5, because the belts are several and only the Pune cohort is proposed.

**Medical devices, 3.645, Medium.** Thirteen tools activate because compliance, mapping and hospital-side payment are all genuine. The score stays Medium because delivery is 3: 4,108 manufacturers with no published state split, so a building site would be a guess. Rating Ki Kaksha is Later: ISO 13485 and the CDSCO class are the discipline buyers ask for; a generic credit-rating clinic is secondary. Lender pathway is Later until a bank in the chosen catchment has a device-collateral view. Business EMI protection is Skip: the stress is regulatory cost and buyer payment, not an EMI product. Evidence: CDSCO’s own statement that production data is not central; DoP export table of USD 4,014 million; PLI limited to 28 applicants, which is why scheme-readiness work is “use the park and state scheme, do not queue for PLI”.

**Technical textiles, 3.485, Medium.** Mapping is 5 because the export number (₹24,732.68 crore) does not identify the firms. Payment support and ODR are Activate on the trading pattern of textile supply, which should be measured in the chosen town rather than assumed from apparel. Scheme usability is Later: the live schemes are PLI and GREAT, and both are the wrong window for a small converter, so the honest “readiness” work is often to say no. Scorecards are Later until the cohort exists. Additionality is 3 because textile research associations already own testing and R&D.

**Toys, 3.385, Medium.** Policy readiness and compliance are 5 because the QCO, from 1 January 2021, is a clean drop-off: DGCIS notes that some small units shut, and BIS licensing is the gate to sell. Friction research is 5 for that reason. Pillar 2 stays in the 2–3 range: the buyer is a retailer or a marketplace more often than a large company with a statutory delayed-payment case, and this review found no payment-cycle statistic. That low Pillar 2, at 24 per cent of the formula, is why a strong Pillar 1 does not produce a High score. Business EMI protection is Skip.

**Defence suppliers, 3.290, Medium.** Mapping and compliance are the work: three population numbers (about 16,000 MSMEs, 462 licensed companies, 619 iDEX startups and MSMEs) cannot be briefed as one cluster. Payment support is 4 because milestone billing is structural, and it is not 5 because this review did not find a published median delay. Market access is Later: empanelment is the DPSU’s process, and SIDM already organises the industry. A centre without a buyer at the table will train vendors for orders it cannot see. ODR is Later: many counterparties and many vendors sit outside the micro and small ceiling of the MSE facilitation route, so the statutory path has to be chosen case by case.

**Micro food processing, 3.240, Medium.** Credit Ki Kaksha, scheme usability and friction research are the strongest in the whole set. The evidence is official and awkward in the right way: 1,72,707 approved and 59,202 formalised, same as-on date, and a credit-linked scheme whose evaluation reports a 1.7 times turnover change without giving a baseline turnover. Rating Ki Kaksha is Skip: an external rating is the wrong instrument for this unit. Payment support, ODR and recovery outcomes are Skip at national level because the buyer is local. Delivery is 2. Additionality is 3 only if the centre studies the approval-to-formalisation leak and prepares files the state nodal agency does not standardise. If it answers the same PMFME helpline, additionality is 2 and the overall score falls below 3.2.

**Electronics component tier, 3.195, Medium.** Only five tools activate. Mapping is 5 because MeitY counts principals and units and does not count the supplier tier. Payment support is 4 conditional on the supplier actually existing and selling to the assembler. Almost every readiness tool is Later, because there is not yet a list of enterprises to be ready. Using mobile-export growth (USD 38.58 billion of electronic goods, or ₹2 lakh crore of mobile exports) as evidence of MSME exporter success would be the category error this report is written to prevent.

**Solar modules, 2.910, Watch.** Three tools at 4: payment support for manufacturer-to-developer terms, mapping of the ALMM list into a cohort definition, and compliance as cell ALMM arrives. Additionality is 2. Delivery is 3 only because Gujarat plants cluster; it is not 4, because those plants are not a microenterprise population. ODR and Business EMI protection are Skip.

**Drones, 2.410, Watch.** Mapping is the only Activate, and it can be a note, not a centre. The July 2024 official counts (48 manufacturing companies, 70 certified models) and the FY2021–22 sales of ₹319 crore across 23 PLI firms are the evidence that the population is too small. Rating, receivables, lender pathway and scorecards are Skip. Namo Drone Didi is not used to inflate delivery fit.

No entry falls below 2. “Out of charter” would require a population the tools cannot touch at all. Screened-out semiconductor, hydrogen, ACC and white-goods projects are the nearest cases, and they were not forced through the formula.

## Section 3 — Selection and delivery

### 3.1 The choice

Open **one** Capability Centre in this round. Attach it to existing auto-component infrastructure in the Pune–Chakan–Talegaon–Ranjangaon belt. Do not found a park, a common facility, or a new industrial estate.

Do not open a second centre on this evidence. Commission one mapping sprint, on medical-device licensees, with a decision gate at the end. Everything else waits.

The charter score and the shape score agree on the first choice. They do not agree on the second: food processing has more micro enterprises than medical devices and a worse place to put a building. The formula correctly refuses to promote it to a national centre. Medical devices is the mapping priority because the manufacturer count already exists (4,108) and the missing fact is geographic, which a sprint can close. Food’s missing fact is which one of 726 districts, and Pillar 2 would still be weak after that choice.

### 3.2 Pune belt — attached centre

**Location and cohort.** A desk inside or beside an existing common facility, association office or SIDBI office in the Pune auto belt. Not a new shed. Cohort: manufacturing enterprises in that belt that sell parts into a vehicle supply chain, are below the PLI Auto applicant list, and opt in. Tyres, batteries, paints, dealers and EV brands are out, consistent with the ACMA scope and with the centre’s actual tools.

**Why this one.** Shape 5 and charter 4.00. Buyers are physically present. The payment-to-credit file is not what the OEM, ACMA or the common facility runs. The belt is dense enough that a cohort can be filled without pretending the centre serves Hosur and Manesar.

**Tools to run immediately (Activate).** Credit Ki Kaksha. Rating Ki Kaksha. Digital Dost. Policy and programme readiness, aimed at CGTMSE, TReDS, ZED and the state scheme, and explicitly away from PLI Auto for firms that cannot qualify. Cluster-led payment support. ODR pathway for micro and small cases that fit the statutory route, as case preparation and referral only. Recovery and repayment outcomes, recorded from opted-in cases. Capital pathways, as working capital matched to the receivable cycle. Scheme usability. Lender pathways, after the file is ready, with the lender making the credit decision. Cluster mapping of the opted-in belt. Friction research on where firms drop out of TReDS, CGTMSE and OEM vendor lists. A scorecard that is allowed to show blank cells.

**Tools to defer or skip.** Market access: Later. Use the OEM and ACMA channels; do not build a parallel fair. Compliance intelligence: Later, unless a live QCO is blocking opted-in firms. Business EMI protection: Later, and only as a structured conversation with lenders about whether EMI stress during schedule cuts is real in this cohort. Do not describe it as a cover that can be bought.

**First 90 days.**

1. Write the cohort rule on one page and publish the exclusion list (PLI applicants, dealers, tyre and battery makers, traders).
2. Confirm the host institution and a data rule: enterprise data comes from firms that opt in. No scraped MCA “census” and no turnover-band claims.
3. Recruit the first batch for Credit Ki Kaksha from firms the host already knows. Target a batch the staff can finish, not a launch number invented for a press note.
4. Open a payment-friction register. Record buyer, invoice age and whether the firm is micro or small, because that fact decides MSE facilitation versus contract. Prepare cases. Refer. Do not adjudicate, collect or promise recovery.
5. Hold a lender session only for firms with a completed file. Lenders keep the credit decision. The centre does not rank lenders.
6. Draft the scorecard with the indicators that can be filled from the register (files completed, cases prepared, days since invoice as reported by the firm) and with explicit blanks for industry turnover bands.
7. At day 90, decide whether the opted-in cohort is dense enough to continue. If it is not, stop, and do not widen the centre to the whole of Maharashtra to fill the room.

**How this differs from the park, the scheme and the anchor.** The common facility sells shared machines, testing and training. The OEM develops vendors it wants to buy from. PLI pays approved applicants for eligible advanced-technology sales. The centre does none of those. It makes a small supplier’s receivables and documents legible to the institutions that already exist, and it keeps the evidence. Neutrality rules of the platform apply: no preferred lender, no lead allocation, no recovery guarantee.

### 3.3 Medical-device mapping sprint — not a centre yet

**Location.** Undecided. The sprint’s only job is to place the 4,108 licensed manufacturers into catchments and to see whether one catchment has all four of: a mass of small licensees, a buyer with repeated payment friction, a park or hospital system that will host a desk, and firms that are not the 28 PLI applicants.

**Tools if a catchment survives the sprint.** The Activate list in §2.4: credit files, digital and policy readiness, payment support, ODR case preparation, recovery tracking, capital and scheme pathways, market access (this is the entry where market access earns a 5, because CE, ICMED and hospital-buyer entry are the growth constraint), mapping, friction research, scorecards, compliance intelligence.

**Not now.** Rating clinics, lender pathways and Business EMI protection, as scored.

**Why it is not selected in this round.** Delivery fit is 3 until the catchment is counted. Selecting Greater Noida or Visakhapatnam from a park announcement would confuse a real-estate project with a cohort.

### 3.5 Dependencies and exclusions

- Host agreement in the Pune belt. Without a desk inside an institution the firms already enter, delivery fit falls and the score should be recomputed.
- Opt-in data. Turnover bands stay blank until firms report them. Publishing a reconstructed band would break the rule in §1.3.
- MSE facilitation and ODR providers remain external. The centre prepares and refers.
- PLI Auto applicants are excluded from the cohort even if they ask to join. Their inclusion would inflate outcomes with firms the incentive already serves.
- No lender onboarding target, no recovery-rate target and no job target in the first 90 days. The day-90 decision is whether the cohort is real.
- Medical sprint depends on a CDSCO or state-licensing source that can be tabulated by district. If that source is refused, the sprint stops. It does not guess a city.
